Incorporating uncertainty
into classifier rankings
A classifier assigns each observation a probability of belonging to a target class. Rather than relying
on a single ordering, repeated model fits or posterior draws can quantify uncertainty in probabilities
and in their relative ranks.


01     Estimate uncertainty in predicted probabilities
For each observation i, obtain B estimates of its class probability. Common approaches include:

Bootstrap                            Resample training data and refit the classifier.

Deep ensembles                       Train multiple independently initialized models.

Bayesian inference                   Draw model parameters from their posterior distribution.

MC dropout                           Repeat inference with dropout enabled.

Gaussian processes                   Use posterior draws from a probabilistic classifier.


   pᵢ⁽¹⁾, pᵢ⁽²⁾, …, pᵢ⁽ᴮ⁾ → mean, standard deviation, quantiles

Calculate the mean, standard deviation and an empirical 95% interval (2.5th and 97.5th percentiles).
These intervals describe uncertainty across the chosen model draws; their coverage is not
automatically guaranteed.

02      Rank using uncertainty-adjusted probabilities
A conservative ranking score can penalize uncertainty in the estimated class probability:

      Sᵢ = μᵢ − λσᵢ

Here μᵢ is the mean predicted probability, σᵢ is its standard deviation, and λ controls risk aversion.
When λ = 0, the score reduces to the mean. Larger values favor more precise estimates.

Alternatively, rank by the lower quantile of the probability distribution, such as the 5th percentile. This
is particularly useful for asymmetric distributions.

  Observation                        Mean    SD             Score (λ=1)            Score (λ=2)

  A                                  0.92    0.12           0.80                   0.68

  B                                  0.87    0.03           0.84                   0.81

  C                                  0.78    0.04           0.74                   0.70

  D                                  0.71    0.13           0.58                   0.45

  E                                  0.52    0.03           0.49                   0.46


The penalty changes the decision objective. If the goal is to maximize the expected number of true
positives in a fixed-size selection, ranking by the mean predictive probability is optimal; a penalty is a
separate risk preference.


03      Estimate uncertainty in the ranking itself
For each bootstrap replicate or posterior draw b, rank all observations together by their predicted
probabilities (highest first). This produces a distribution of ranks for each observation.

      rᵢ⁽ᵇ⁾ = descending rank of pᵢ⁽ᵇ⁾

Summarize this distribution using expected rank, a percentile interval for rank, probability of ranking
first, and probability of ranking in the top k. Specify how ties are handled.

04         Calculate pairwise outranking probabilities
Estimate how often observation i has a higher predicted probability than observation j across the
same B model draws:

        P(pᵢ > pⱼ) ≈ (1/B) Σᵦ 1[pᵢ⁽ᵇ⁾ > pⱼ⁽ᵇ⁾]

For example, if A outranks B in 60% of model draws, their relative order is uncertain even if A has the
higher mean probability. Compare observations within the same model draw to preserve dependence
between predictions. Bootstrap frequencies describe model-fit variability rather than literal Bayesian
posterior probabilities.

Practical recommendation: report mean probabilities alongside rank intervals, top-k frequencies and
pairwise comparisons. Use uncertainty penalties only when justified by the decision objective.
