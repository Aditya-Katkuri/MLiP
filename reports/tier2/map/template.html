How the ranking works

Each barrier is a Project Sidewalk label cluster: one physical feature, however many volunteers marked it. Only clusters inside the city boundary are ranked.

priority = magnitude × validity × (0.2 + 0.8 × demand)
  • Magnitude uses Project Sidewalk's own published access-score constants: base weight × severity multiplier, plus tag adjustments. That makes a severity-3 missing ramp with "no alternate route" 1.5, and a severity-3 obstacle or surface problem 1.0. Curb ramps enter only when their score is negative, meaning a deficient ramp.
  • Validity uses human votes only. It is 1.0 if validators agreed, 0.8 if unvalidated, and 0.6 if also labelled by a low-quality account. Barriers humans voted down are dropped (86). SidewalkAI votes are ignored.
  • Demand is the weighted average of four 0–1 percentile scores: weekday transit trips within 400 m (0.30), weighted destinations within 400 m (0.30), a tract vulnerability index of residents 65+, disability, no car and density (0.25), and pedestrian crashes within 100 m or the High Injury Network (0.15).
  • Two lists. Spot repairs and missing sidewalks are ranked separately. A cracked ramp is maintenance, an absent sidewalk is new construction, and Project Sidewalk's magnitudes for the two are not on one scale.

How much to trust it

No ranking has ground truth. The evaluation instead measures how much the order moves when each judgment call changes. The table shows the share of the default top 100 still in the top 100 under each change.
